ÀNEC AMB PERES

Catalan duck braised with firm autumn pears, a dark sofregit and an almond–garlic picada

Prep 35 min Cook 2 h 35 min Total 3 h 25 min Serves 6

WHAT YOU NEED

12 g for the duck + 2 g to finish the sauce

PREP LISTdo it while the duck rests (step 1)

  • Grate the tomatoes and discard the skins
  • Peel the pears and core them from the base, stems on
  • Heat the stock and keep it hot

EQUIPMENT

HOW TO MAKE IT

follow the arrows
1

Season the duck

DUCK
  • Pat the pieces dry. Trim loose flaps of fat, but leave the skin on.
  • Season all over with 12 g salt and the 2 g pepper.
  • Leave for 30 min – work through the prep list meanwhile.
  • Keep legs, wings and breasts in separate piles: they go into the braise at different times.
Ask the butcher to cut the duck into 8 pieces through the joints – it cooks evenly and leaves fewer small bones in the sauce.
2

Render & brown

DUCK
  • Wide heavy casserole over medium-low heat.
  • Duck skin-side down, without crowding: 10–12 min, until plenty of fat has rendered and the skin is a deep burnished gold.
  • Turn and brown the flesh side 3 min. Repeat in batches – add the olive oil only if the pan is dry.
  • Move to a tray, keeping the breasts apart.
Pour the excess fat through a fine sieve and refrigerate it (great for potatoes). Leave about 30 ml in the pan for the sofregit.
3

Build the sofregit

SOFREGIT
  • Lower the heat. Cook the 400 g onions in the duck fat for 30–35 min, stirring often, until dark gold, soft and jammy.
  • Add the 2 sliced garlic cloves; cook 1 min.
  • Stir in the 250 g grated tomato; cook 12–15 min until reduced to a thick rust-coloured paste with the fat showing at its edges.
  • Pour in the 100 ml vi ranci, scrape the casserole clean and reduce by half.
4

Braise the legs

DUCKBRAISE
  • Return only the legs and wings to the casserole.
  • Add the bay leaf, thyme, cinnamon and 600 ml hot stock (keep the other 150 ml hot for step 8).
  • The liquid should reach about halfway up the duck – not cover it.
  • Bring to a quiet simmer, set the lid slightly ajar and cook 50 min.
  • Turn the pieces once, without stirring the sofregit up from the bottom.
Keep it at a quiet tremble, not a boil. Start step 5 and step 6 now so the pears and picada are ready when the 50 minutes are up.
WHILE THE LEGS BRAISE – do these two side by side
5

Poach the pears

PEARS
  • Peeled pears + 1.25 L water + 15 ml lemon juice in the saucepan.
  • Bring just to a simmer; poach gently 12–18 min, turning once.
  • Test at 12 min: a fine skewer enters the outer flesh but meets clear resistance near the centre.
  • Lift out carefully and drain upright. They must still be firm – they finish in the duck sauce.
Conference or Bosc need closer to 18 min; a small Blanquilla only 10–12. Test the fruit, not the clock.
6

Pound the picada

PICADA
  • Pound the 40 g toasted almonds, 20 g toasted bread, 2 garlic cloves and 10 g parsley to a close, slightly coarse paste.
  • Work in about 75 ml hot liquid from the casserole to loosen it.
  • Using a food processor? Stop before the almonds turn oily and smooth – the picada should keep a little grain.
7

Finish the braise

DUCKPEARSPICADA

A · AFTER THE 50 MIN

  • Add the browned breasts skin-side up.
  • Simmer gently 15 min.

B · PICADA + PEARS

  • Stir the loosened picada into the sauce around the duck, then nestle in the pears.
  • Cook uncovered 15–20 min, spooning sauce over the pears twice.

C · CHECK

  • Sauce clings lightly to a spoon.
  • Breasts reach at least 74 °C near the bone.
  • A leg joint moves easily when pressed.
Legs still tight once the breasts are cooked? Lift out the breasts and pears, cover them, and give the legs another 10–15 min. Duck follows tenderness, not pride.
8

Rest & serve

+ 2 g SALTRESERVED STOCK
  • Remove the bay leaf, thyme stems and cinnamon.
  • Rest 15 min, then skim away any large pools of fat.
  • Stir in the last 2 g salt. Loosen with a little reserved hot stock only if the sauce has become too thick.
  • Serve one pear per portion of duck; spoon the glossy picada-bound sauce over both.
Serve with pa de pagès for the sauce, and a medium-bodied Garnatxa or Garnatxa-Samsó red from the Empordà.

GOOD TO KNOW

SWAPS

  • No vi ranci: 75 ml dry oloroso sherry + 25 ml water – nuttier, less caramel-like. Never a sweet cream sherry.
  • Pears should smell ripe but stay firm at the neck. Blanquilla is traditional; Conference or Bosc hold their shape better. A fully ripe Bartlett turns to purée.
  • No whole duck: 6 large duck legs (1.8–2 kg). Braise about 75 min, then add the picada and pears and finish 15–20 min.
  • Use unsalted stock – the sauce concentrates a lot. Taste it, don't guess.

MAKE AHEAD

  • Toast the almonds and bread up to 2 days ahead; cool and keep airtight. Make the picada on the day – raw garlic turns harsh as it sits.
  • Cook the duck and sauce 1 day ahead, without the pears. Chill covered, lift off the firm fat, reheat at a bare simmer, then add fresh picada and pears for the final 15 min.
  • The finished dish keeps 3 days in the fridge. Reheat gently and turn the pears as little as possible – they soften each time.
